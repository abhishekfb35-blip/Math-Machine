You’re not wrong. On mobile, your current top horizontal filter bar *is* starting to feel overloaded.

I checked the page structure and the issue is not the existence of horizontal filters themselves — those are still the right direction for TurtleLittle. The issue is **density + hierarchy collapse**.

Your desktop logic is being compressed into a mobile width without enough prioritization.

Right now mobile has these problems:

* too many chips competing equally
* insufficient visual hierarchy
* high cognitive scan cost
* chips wrapping / stacking creates visual noise
* product grid starts too late below the fold
* every filter *looks primary*

For a theme-heavy catalog like TurtleLittle, this becomes dangerous because users stop parsing and start ignoring.

Big marketplaces like [Amazon India](https://www.amazon.in?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and [Flipkart](https://www.flipkart.com?utm_source=chatgpt.com) avoid this by making mobile filters:

* condensed
* progressive
* contextual
* partially hidden behind drawers/sheets

—not because horizontal bars are bad, but because mobile attention bandwidth is tiny.

What I’d recommend for TurtleLittle:

---

## Keep ONLY 4–6 high-value quick filters visible horizontally

Example:

* Age
* Gender
* Theme
* Occasion
* Price
* Sort

That’s it.

Everything else should go inside:

* a bottom sheet
* “More Filters”
* expandable modal

---

## Your current mistake: treating all filters as equal

This is the core UX issue.

For your business, filters have different strategic roles:

| Type             | Importance      |
| ---------------- | --------------- |
| Theme            | HIGH            |
| Age/Gender       | HIGH            |
| Occasion         | HIGH            |
| Style            | MEDIUM          |
| Material         | LOW             |
| Operational tags | INTERNAL mostly |

But mobile UI currently exposes too much at once.

Users don’t want taxonomy.
They want narrowing.

---

## What works better for YOUR catalog

You have ~35 themes.

That immediately means:

### DON’T:

Show many themes as chips at top.

### DO:

Show:

* 3–5 most relevant themes
* then “More”

Example:

```text
🧸 Cartoon
🦄 Unicorn
🚗 Cars
⚽ Sports
+24 More
```

This is critical.

Because:

* relevance-first beats completeness
* mobile discovery is associative, not exhaustive
* users rarely browse 35 visible chips

---

## Another issue:

Your chip styling currently has similar visual weight everywhere.

You need stronger hierarchy.

Suggested:

### Level 1 (sticky top row)

Large pills:

* Theme
* Age
* Occasion
* Sort

### Level 2

Inside bottom sheet:

* all detailed filters
* advanced narrowing

---

## The biggest mobile ecommerce mistake:

Trying to replicate desktop filtering.

Desktop:

* exploratory
* wide scan behavior

Mobile:

* guided narrowing
* fast elimination

Those are different UX systems.

---

## What I’d do structurally

### MOBILE LAYOUT

```text
[Search]

[Theme ▼] [Age ▼] [Occasion ▼] [Sort ▼]

----------------

Showing: Kids Towels

Popular themes:
[Animals] [Space] [Cars] [+More]

----------------

Products grid
```

NOT:

```text
50 chips before products
```

That kills momentum.

---

## Another important point

You’re selling emotional products.

That means:

* inspiration matters more than filtering precision
* browsing flow matters more than taxonomy completeness

Amazon-style dense filtering is NOT ideal for you.

Pinterest-lite + commerce is closer to your category psychology.

---

## Final verdict

Your instinct is correct:
the current mobile top filter area is visually too busy.

But don’t swing to the opposite extreme and move everything into hidden side drawers like desktop Amazon.

For TurtleLittle:

* horizontal quick filters = good
* too many visible chips = bad
* aggressive prioritization = necessary
* progressive disclosure = essential

You need a:

> “guided discovery” mobile UX

—not a “full catalog control panel.”
