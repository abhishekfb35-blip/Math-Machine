Yes — but only partially. The current “See All” links on [TurtleLittle](https://turtlelittle.com?utm_source=chatgpt.com) are functional, not optimal. ([TurtleLittle][1])

What’s working:

* They appear after each themed product block, which is predictable.
* They help reduce homepage clutter.
* They reinforce category segmentation (Girls Towels, Boys Towels, Bathrobes, etc.).

But there are some UX and conversion issues:

### 1. “Click to See All…” feels dated

That wording looks like old Magento-era ecommerce.

Instead of:

* “Click to See All Girls Towels!”

Use:

* “View All Girls Towels”
* “Shop All Girls Towels”
* “Explore Collection”

Cleaner. More premium. Less noisy.

---

### 2. The links are visually weak

Right now they feel like an afterthought beneath the grid.

That is dangerous because:

* users visually stop at the products
* many won’t continue deeper
* category discovery becomes dependent on scrolling

You actually WANT category exploration because your catalog depth is large.

Better:

* make it a proper button
* place it aligned with section title
* or add one at top-right of section

Example:

```text
Girls Towels                         View All →
```

This pattern performs much better because users see the escape route before browsing products.

---

### 3. The homepage is doing too much vertical stacking

You have:

* Girls Towels
* Boys Towels
* Couple Towels
* Boys Blankets
* Girls Blankets
* Bathrobes
  etc. ([TurtleLittle][1])

That becomes endless-scroll fatigue.

The “See All” links become even more important because users won’t reach lower sections.

You should strongly consider:

* fewer homepage sections
* stronger category entry points
* horizontal carousels
* themed landing pages

Otherwise users keep browsing passively instead of navigating intentionally.

---

### 4. “See All” should probably include product counts

This matters more than people think.

Example:

* “View All Girls Towels (148)”

This communicates:

* depth
* authority
* confidence
* assortment size

Without counts, users subconsciously assume the category is small.

---

### 5. Your category hierarchy itself may need rethinking

Right now categories are heavily gendered:

* Girls Towels
* Boys Towels

But your actual buying intent is often:

* cartoon/theme based
* age based
* gifting occasion
* personalization style

Parents usually search by:

* Peppa Pig
* Unicorn
* Marvel
* Princess
* Return gifts
* Baby shower
* School towels

not “boys towel”.

So the “See All” links may be reinforcing a taxonomy that is operationally convenient but not discovery-optimal.

This is especially important since earlier you mentioned you have ~35 themes. Your navigation architecture matters more than your product count now.

A stronger structure could be:

* Characters
* Themes
* Age
* Occasion
* Product Type

with gender becoming a soft filter, not a primary browsing axis.

---

So overall:

* acceptable technically
* weak strategically
* outdated visually
* underutilized as a navigation mechanism

You should treat those links as category-entry CTAs, not as footer text beneath product grids.
