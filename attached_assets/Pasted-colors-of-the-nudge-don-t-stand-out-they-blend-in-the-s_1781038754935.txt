You are entirely correct; the "Buy 2 Get 1 Free" promotional nudge completely blends into the site's layout because it uses the exact same core brand colors (forest green and light grey) as the surrounding static content.
This design choice significantly undermines the visual hierarchy of the shopping page, suppressing user action.
## Why the Nudge Fails to Stand Out

* Color Competition: The active steps and checkmarks utilize the exact same forest green found in the brand's turtle logo and the prominent "Add to Cart" button. The user's eye naturally skips past them because they look like standard, non-interactive site decoration rather than an active promotion.
* Low Contrast Status Indicators: The inactive elements—such as the "Free*" icon, "30% Off*", and "Best Rates!" options—are styled in a very faint, washed-out grey. This treatment makes them look completely disabled or broken rather than incentives meant to drive higher average order values.
* Weak Text Emphasis: The critical line of text prompting action ("Add a 3rd item to unlock 1 FREE Item!") is rendered in a tiny, thin font. It lacks the bold formatting, distinct coloring, or visual weight needed to arrest a scanning customer's attention.

## UX Recommendations to Increase Conversions
To ensure this crucial cart builder tool successfully converts users, consider implementing these interface styling shifts:

[ Current Design ]                 [ Recommended Design ]
Grey/Green Progress Bar    --->    Contrasting Accent Color (e.g., Deep Amber / Coral)
Faint Grey Text            --->    High-Contrast, Bold Text
Standard Text Prompt       --->    Highlighted Callout / Micro-Banner Container


* Introduce an Accent Color: Shift the progress tracker, active checkmarks, and milestone text away from the brand's primary green. Introducing a warm contrast color like deep amber (#D97706) or a vibrant coral will instantly distinguish the promotion from static design elements.
* Containerize the Call-to-Action: Group the line of text ("Awesome! You have 2 items...") and its milestone bar inside a gently shaded background card block. A subtle, warm-tinted background wrapper immediately signals to the user that this specific section requires attention.
* Increase Text Weight & Visual Scaling: Bold the key value proposition words (e.g., "3rd item" and "FREE Item!"). Boosting the text container size ensures that visitors scrolling quickly on mobile devices won't miss the financial incentive.
